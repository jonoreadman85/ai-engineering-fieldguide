PROMPTING · 5 MINUTES

Write prompts that get useful answers

Learn a simple way to turn a vague request into a prompt that gives AI the information and direction it needs to answer well.

The idea

A prompt is a work request. The AI can only use the goal, context, instructions, and constraints you give it, plus what it already knows. A useful prompt closes the gaps that matter for this particular task.

ELI5 · PICTURE THIS

Ordering a custom cake

Imagine ordering a cake by saying only “cake.” The baker has to guess the occasion, size, flavour, and what counts as finished. A prompt is like a short order: tell the AI what you need and the details that change the result.

Map it back: Goal = occasion; context = guests and preferences; task = bake; output = size and design; quality bar = the cake meets the request.

Where it stops: An AI is not a baker who can ask useful questions by default. If a missing detail matters, invite it to ask before guessing.

Use five helpful parts: goal, context, task, output, quality bar. They are a checklist, not a formula: include the parts that would change the answer. For a simple question, one sentence may be enough.

A prompt checklist

Goal: What outcome or decision do I need?

Context: What background, source material, audience, or situation matters?

Task: What should it do now?

Output: What form should the answer take?

Quality bar: What would make the answer useful, accurate, and complete?

See it across different tasks

Writing

Goal: Explain the rollout plan so engineers know what to do next.
Context: Here are the key decisions and open issues: [notes]. The audience is the engineering team.
Task: Draft a concise update.
Output: Headline, decision, next steps, and owners.
Quality bar: Don’t invent decisions; flag missing owners.

Research

Goal: Understand the main approaches to evaluating coding agents.
Context: We are choosing an evaluation method for an internal pilot.
Task: Compare current approaches using primary sources.
Output: A short comparison table with links.
Quality bar: Separate source-backed findings from your interpretation, and note limitations.

Engineering planning

Goal: Find a suitable first workflow to try with an AI agent.
Context: Our team builds [product] using [tools/processes]; constraints include [risks].
Task: Suggest one bounded workflow and identify assumptions.
Output: A short experiment proposal.
Quality bar: Include a way to measure value and a clear point for human review.

The principle: Give the AI the details that would materially change its answer. Don’t make a simple request complicated; add structure when the task or stakes call for it.

TRY IT · 2 MINUTES

Improve a real prompt

Think of a real task you want AI to help with. Draft a prompt that names your goal, gives relevant context, asks for a specific output, and says what a good answer looks like. It can be about work, writing, research, or something you want to learn.

Then iterate

Your first prompt is a starting point. Read the answer against your goal. If it misses, give targeted feedback: name what is missing, provide any new context, and say what to change. Asking for “a better answer” gives the AI little to work with.

There is no universal “best prompt.” The best prompt is the shortest clear request that gives this AI tool enough information to do this task well.

Terms to keep

Quality bar
Observable conditions that tell you whether the answer is good enough to use.
Context
Background information that changes what a good answer should say.
Iteration
Improving a prompt or answer through focused follow-up instructions.
